# Code of Conduct

All members of this course have the following responsibilities as learners, facilitators of learning, and as participants in dialog and discussion. This document was inspired by and references the [p5.js community statement.](https://github.com/processing/p5.js/blob/master/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md)

## As Learners

- Actively ask for help from your peers.
- Be open to new experiences and ideas.
- Be present. Avoid using laptops and phones during discussions and critiques- note that this does not apply to people who require these devices for accessibility reasons.
- Take responsibility and agency in your learning. The objective of this class is to develop knowledge, not apps. Code is a medium for critical thought, but you can't think about something you never actually read or write yourself. 

## As Facilitators of Learning

- Do not assume prior knowledge or imply that there are things someone else should know.
- Actively seek to support your peers when they need help.
- Recognize and validate multiple forms of contributions and expertise.
- Be open to constructive feedback.
- Avoid jargon when possible.

## In Discussions and Critiques

- Prioritize listening.
- Be open to new ideas and alternative perspectives.
- Be mindful and kind in your interactions with others.
- Be respectful. Debate across different points of view is productive and passionate discussion is encouraged; however, disagreement is not an opportunity to attack someone else.
- Be helpfully critical, consider what someone is trying to achieve in their work and how you might provide feedback to aid them in their goals.
- Any form of harassment or offensive comments relating to gender identity and expression, sexual orientation, race, ethnicity, language, neuro-type, size, ability, class, religion, culture, subculture, political opinion, age, skill level, occupation, or background is unacceptable.

## As Authors or users of Computationally-Generated Content

- Openly share your knowledge and progress with your peers. Publish your in-progress and finished work on the course Github organization.
- Give credit to your (human) sources. (See below for details on work generated with large-language models).
- If you are unable to credit a source due to using a tool that obscures training data, provide documentation of any and all input you provided.
- Experimentation, failures, and bugs of your own making are neccesary steps for learning. These kinds of mistakes are encouraged. Slop is not. Don't make slop.

## Policy on LLM use

In this course, we will look at the impact of generative technologies on human expression. These technologies include Large Language Model (LLM) based tools for generating code and images. You will have the opportunity to investigate such technologies over the course of this class, and depending on your chosen assignments, there may be cases in which the inclusion and discussion of images, text, and code generated by such tools is warranted.  In these cases, you must indicate your use of these tools.


### Specific guidance is as follows:

#### When writing prose:
 
**Policy:** Using AI software to write or paraphrase prose text for any assignment and presenting this text as your own writing is not allowed. You may use grammar editing tools like Grammarly, but please note that even these tools are blurring the line with regard to text generation features vs grammar checking. I discourage even the process of using LLM-based tools to restructure text you have written for "clarity". 

**Rationale:**
Writing is (1) a tool for active thought and reflection on behalf of the writer, and (2) a method for communication between two or more humans. If we delegate this task to an automated system, we deprive ourselves of the opportunity to build and refine our critical reasoning ability. Moreover, the things we write in this class are meant to be read and understood by other students in the class. It is both demeaning and disrespectful to give your peers with stochastically extruded text and expect them to intellectually engage with it. The writing requirements for this class are minimal. If extenuating circumstances mean that you don't have time to read a paper in the class, don't enter a reading reflection. (I give everyone a pass on missing at least one.) For more details on my reasoning here see: [https://www.nature.com/articles/s44222-025-00323-4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44222-025-00323-4)

#### When writing code
**Policy:** All students are responsible for submitting assignments with code that is **commented** and with functionality they are **capable of explaining to another person.** Using LLMs to generate or alter programming code under these requirements for any assignment is permitted only if the following conditions are met: 
1. Include in-line comments in your source code about how and why you used LLMs in developing your application. This includes what specific tools were used (Copilot, Claude), any and all prompts provided to the tools, and your justification in using the LLM-based tool. 
2. You read and understand code that was generated. You may be asked at any point, to verbally or textually explain what some code from your assignment does and why, and you will be evaluated on your response.

**Rationale:** In addition to the unique capability of being automatically executable and maintainable by computers, code is very similar to writing prose in that it is (1) a tool for active thought and reflection and (2) a social project for conveying meaning and benefit within and between communities. *Coding computer applications is a means to stimulate creative thought and dialog, not the other way around.* When you delegate code generation and verification to an LLM, you largely eliminate the opportunity for cognition and understanding of the underlying functionality of what you are trying to build, and how your implementation aligns with or diverges from your design objectives. If you choose to use LLMs in this context, then you must demonstrate that you have considered why this is justified, and put in additional effort to clarify how the tools have been used. I recognize that you can use LLMs to generate the in-line comments that are required by point 1 in the above policy, which is also why point 2 is there. 

**But don't I need to learn to use AI coding tools to be competitive in the job market?** Within this class, we are not a commercial software team, you are not under production deadlines to ship code, and you in no way are rewarded for number of commits or lines of code written. This course is structured so that a student with minimal prior programming expertise can achieve a high grade without the use of LLM tools (many students have achieved this in the past!) Furthermore, if your motivation is to build technical skills (which is a great one!), it is unlikely given [current studies](https://www.softwareseni.com/the-evidence-against-vibe-coding-what-research-reveals-about-ai-code-quality/), that vibecoding will lead you to accomplish this objective. You have to be able to [<u>debug</u>](https://adventures.nodeland.dev/archive/the-human-in-the-loop/).

#### When creating images
Using AI software to generate images / video / other media elements as the output of your work is permitted; however, you are required to list the tool used and any and all prompts you provided to generate the resulting imagery. In these cases, the prompts will be considered the work you did for the assignment and your effort will be graded accordingly. Further, the legality of using copyrighted training data for these tools is currently an [unsettled matter](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/09/27/artificial-intelligence-day-of-action-us-congress-copyright-regulation), such technologies frequently rely on [low-paid under-resourced humans](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2023/08/28/scale-ai-remotasks-philippines-artificial-intelligence/) to function, and they are terrible for the [environment and natural resource use](https://www.lincolninst.edu/publications/land-lines-magazine/articles/land-water-impacts-data-centers/).

This policy is subject to change.
