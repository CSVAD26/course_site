# Project 2 - Data Portrait

## Due Tuesday, November 10, 2026

Adapted from Quantified Selfie Project in [Code as a Creative Medium](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262542043/code-as-creative-medium/)

## Project Requirements

Create a visualization that presents insights from one or more personal datasets about yourself. You may use pre-existing datasets (e.g., your email archive, photo album metadata, fitness data from a tracking app) or create a new system for collecting data (potentially using analog sensors). You may use an expansive definition of what constitutes data by either focusing on technologically quantifiable information (e.g., the amount of time spent on your phone per week) or more unconventional phenomena collected manually (e.g., the number and location of tar spots on your feet from walking at Coal Oil Point). The scale of the dataset is up to you.

Your project must satisfy the following elements:

- Engage in all aspects of the data visualization pipeline, including data acquisition, parsing, cleaning, and representation. Note that not all of these aspects must be present in the final application- e.g., you may use an existing process to generate a dataset and then write a p5.js program that parses and visualizes the resulting data. Note that while you have to have SOME interactive visualization mechanism in your project, you might choose to invest the bulk of your effort into creating the data acquisition pipeline.
- Present an interactive data visualization. Your resulting application must enable the user to explore or filter the dataset through one or more interactive operations. Example operations include, but are not limited to, zooming, sorting, filtering, and querying.
- Present a unifying information aesthetics that makes your data beautiful, interpretable, and/or conveys a specific narrative

## Inspirational Starting Points

- Create a personal report on quantifiable aspects of your life from a given time period ([see Nick Felton's Annual reports 2005,2006,2007,2008,2013](http://feltron.com/))
- Develop a new method for capturing personal data by analyzing patterns in your daily life. (see [Rachel Binx's WiFi Diary](https://rachelbinx.com/art/wifidiary))
- Develop a visualization to display aspects of your local environment. (see [Cy Keener's Remote Winds](https://www.cykeener.com/#/remote-winds/))

## Project Deliverables

- Source code with comments uploaded to GitHub. NOTE: You are not required to submit your source data to GitHub, nor should you if it constitutes sensitive personal information. See [setting up a .gitignore file](https://docs.github.com/en/get-started/git-basics/ignoring-files)
- Complied working version of the app brought to class for review.
- Description of project functionality and design principles in accompanying [README.md](http://readme.md) file, also uploaded to GitHub. Minimum 500 words describing your process, reflections, and outcomes.

## Review of Project

In Groups of 2, let your partner try out your data visualization interface. One person will act as the "observer", the other person will act as the "user". Observers will give the user their tool to try out. Observers can respond to direct questions from the user, but otherwise, you cannot intervene.  Users- during your exploration, note out loud any observations you have about the data being visualized.

Spend 5 minutes engaged with the interface, then switch.

Engage in a short discussion with your partner (7 minutes).

What, if anything, did you learn about the type of data being visualized?

- What was aesthetically compelling about the interaction/visualization?
- What features were lacking from the interface that you feel might be helpful to add in the future?

Follow this with a joint group discussion to discuss the results of the observation.
